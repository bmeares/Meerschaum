## Notes (added by hand after the run)

- Cases: `sync_*` is a fresh destination; `inc_*` appends 1% new source rows to an already synced table; `resync_100k` re-runs `sync` on a fully synced 100k pipe whose source did not change (every fetched row is a duplicate, so the time is the fetch plus the `filter_existing` diff); `filter_existing_100k` is the Python `pipe.filter_existing()` call on a 100k frame with 1% new rows; `get_data_*` is `pipe.get_data()` of the whole table. Python columns are timed around the call; the `startup_*` rows are whole-process times of fresh interpreters or CLI processes. v3 and v4 CLIs are `python -m meerschaum`; `v3cli`, `v4cli` and `v5cli` were measured for startup only, the standalone binary (`v5bin`) also for the sync, incremental and re-sync cases.
- The `{{ Pipe() }}` chain, dependency-engine and per-call latency cases of earlier reports were not run in this comparison.
- Startup medians come from a second invocation of the harness (the PostGIS run, which re-measures startup and replaces the first). The first invocation measured v3/v4/v5 `import meerschaum` at 0.195/0.199/0.156 s and `--version` at 0.198/0.200/0.033 s (v3 CLI, v4 CLI, v5 wheel CLI), consistent with the table.
- v4 SQLite 1M fresh sync is bimodal. The three runs of this report were 413, 287 and 414 s (median 413 s); a separate five-run confirmation (`2026-10-20-v4-sqlite-1M-extra.json`, same artifacts and harness) gave 292, 301, 412, 290 and 402 s. Both modes appear on an otherwise idle machine, so the cause is in v4 itself or its SQLite use rather than host noise (not diagnosed); the v5/v4 ratio for that single cell is 0.02 with the report's median and about 0.03 with the faster mode. v4 `inc_1M` on SQLite is also noisy (42 to 54 s). The v5 samples are tight (spread about 3% or less on the 1M sync cells).
- v3 and v4 `import meerschaum` in a completely fresh root run through a one-time recursive config/virtual-environment initialization (a RecursionError and a stale venv lock, 4 to 7 s). The harness initializes every v3 and v4 root before measuring (untimed `bootstrap`, two consecutive clean imports required); v5 has no such step.
- v3 and v4 pipes were registered by the v4 interpreter (the harness registers every implementation's pipes once per backend); v3 read them without error and every v3 run passed the harness's row-count verification.
- v5 `filter_existing_100k` and `get_data_*` are Python-only cases and have no CLI counterpart.
